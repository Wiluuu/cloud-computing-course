Throughout Mission 3, exploring the big three public cloud platforms—Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform—provided valuable perspective on modern cloud architecture. 

The cloud platform that impressed me the most was Google Cloud Platform (GCP). Its native focus on containerization through Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) and its high-performance analytics capabilities clearly demonstrate why it leads in AI and data science fields. 

Despite their architectural differences, AWS, Azure, and GCP share fundamental similarities. All three base their delivery models on core virtualized pillars: compute virtual machines, scalable object storage, private cloud networking, managed relational databases, and identity/access management. Each provider also emphasizes elastic scaling, pay-as-you-go billing, and geographic redundancy.

This activity highlighted that business requirements—not just brand popularity—must drive platform selection. An organization heavily invested in Windows ecosystems benefits far more from Azure, while an AI research laboratory benefits more from GCP's compute hardware.

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant taught me to evaluate business constraints like existing IT stacks, budgets, and growth projections before making infrastructure decisions. Continuing to build my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio with structured Markdown documentation, architectural decision matrices, and lab evidence enhances my technical communication skills and strengthens my practical readiness as an IT professional.
